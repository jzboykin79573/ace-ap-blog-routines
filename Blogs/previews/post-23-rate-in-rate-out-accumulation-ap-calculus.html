The Part Nobody Tells You

Most students who get a 2 or a 3 on the AP Calculus exam can integrate. They can evaluate definite integrals. They can handle the algebra. That part is not the problem.

The problem is that they do not know what question the integral is answering.

The AP exam writes a story about water flowing into and out of a tank, or cars entering and leaving a parking lot. Then it asks you 4 specific questions about that story. Students who understand integration mechanics but have never seen this framing miss every point that requires interpretation and justification. The math is correct. The reading is wrong. That is where the points go.

This FRQ is not an integration test. It is a reading comprehension test with calculus wrapped around it.

Rate in/rate out problems appear on nearly every released AP Calculus AB and BC exam. They show up in Parts (a), (b), (c), and (d) of the free response, and they always ask the same 4 things. Once you know the framework, you can spot those 4 questions from a mile away and write the answer before you even finish reading the problem.

This post gives you the equation that runs every accumulation FRQ, both problem types (table and formula), all 4 sub-question types with full rubric breakdowns, and the 3 mistakes that cost points every year. You will know exactly what the AP grader is looking for and how to write it.

What Rate In/Rate Out Actually Means

Think about a bank account. You start with a balance. Money comes in. Money goes out. At any point, your balance is the starting amount plus everything that came in minus everything that went out.

That is the entire concept.

An integral is a continuous version of adding up all those deposits and withdrawals. When the rate of change is a smooth function of time, we use integration instead of arithmetic to find the running total.

Here is the connection worth memorizing: $R_{in}(t) - R_{out}(t)$ is the derivative of the total quantity $Q(t)$. In other words, the net rate function is the instantaneous rate of change of the quantity. The integral of that rate function is the total accumulation. This is the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus applied to a real-world context, which is exactly what Unit 8 is testing.

The net change theorem written as an accumulation equation:

$$Q(b) = Q(a) + \int_a^b \left[R_{in}(t) - R_{out}(t)\right] dt$$

$Q(a)$ is the starting amount. The integral is the net change over $[a, b]$. $Q(b)$ is the ending amount.

Students see this problem type in a word problem and assume the setup is more complicated than it looks. It is not. That equation above is always the starting point. The only things that change between problems are the formulas (or table values) for $R_{in}$ and $R_{out}$ and the specific question being asked.

The Framework: One Equation, 4 Sub-Questions

Every rate in/rate out FRQ on the AP exam asks essentially the same 4 things. The numbers and context change. The structure does not.

1
Find the amount at time $t$

Direct application of the net change theorem: $Q(t) = Q(0) + \int_0^t [R_{in}(u) - R_{out}(u)]\,du$. Evaluate the integral. Add the initial condition. Do not forget $Q(0)$.

2
When is the quantity at its maximum (or minimum)?

The quantity is increasing when $R_{in}(t) > R_{out}(t)$ and decreasing when $R_{in}(t) < R_{out}(t)$. The maximum occurs where the net rate changes from positive to negative. Set $R_{in}(t) = R_{out}(t)$ and solve for $t$. Verify the sign change. If the critical point is outside the domain, check the endpoints.

3
Net change vs. total accumulated

These are different and the AP exam tests whether you know the difference.

Net change: $\int_a^b [R_{in}(t) - R_{out}(t)]\,dt$ — how much more or less is in the tank than at the start.

Total amount that entered: $\int_a^b R_{in}(t)\,dt$ — how much poured in, regardless of what also left.

"How much is in the tank at $t = b$?" requires net change. "How much total water entered?" requires $R_{in}$ only.

4
Is the quantity increasing or decreasing at time $t$? Justify.

Evaluate $R_{in}(t) - R_{out}(t)$ at the given time. State whether the result is positive or negative. Interpret the sign in terms of the context. All three pieces are required for full credit.

The decision tree: table vs. formula

Problem gives a TABLE

Approximate the integral using a Riemann sum. The problem will specify which sum (left, right, or midpoint). Use exactly what is asked. Do not try to fit a formula and integrate.

Problem gives FORMULAS

Compute the integral exactly. Set up the antiderivative, evaluate at the bounds, and simplify. Do not use a Riemann sum when an exact answer is possible.

Using the wrong method loses the setup point. The grader cannot award consistency points for a work chain that started with the wrong approach.

Worked Example 1: Table-Based Problem

A water tank contains 500 gallons at time $t = 0$. Water flows into the tank at rate $R_{in}(t)$ (gallons per hour) and out at rate $R_{out}(t)$. Selected values are given in the table.

$t$ (hours)$R_{in}(t)$$R_{out}(t)$
02012
21815
42218
61620
81422

Part (a)

Use a left Riemann sum with 4 subintervals to approximate the total amount of water that entered the tank from $t = 0$ to $t = 8$.

This question asks about total water entering. Integrate $R_{in}$ only. The 4 subintervals are $[0,2]$, $[2,4]$, $[4,6]$, $[6,8]$, each with width $\Delta t = 2$. A left Riemann sum uses the left endpoint of each interval.

$$\int_0^8 R_{in}(t)\,dt \approx (2)(20) + (2)(18) + (2)(22) + (2)(16)$$
$$= 40 + 36 + 44 + 32 = 152 \text{ gallons}$$

Approximately 152 gallons entered the tank from $t = 0$ to $t = 8$.

Here is exactly how the AP exam scores this type of problem.
  • 1 point: correct setup (left sum with $\Delta t = 2$ and left endpoints from the table)
  • 1 point: correct computation, or a computation consistent with your setup

Show the full sum. Writing only "152" without the sum structure loses the setup point even if the arithmetic is right.

Part (b)

Approximate how much water is in the tank at $t = 8$.

Now we need the amount in the tank, not total inflow. Use the net change theorem with both rates. Compute the net rate at each left endpoint:

Left endpoint $t$$R_{in}(t)$$R_{out}(t)$Net rate
020128
218153
422184
61620-4

Left Riemann sum for the net rate:

$$\int_0^8 [R_{in}(t) - R_{out}(t)]\,dt \approx (2)(8) + (2)(3) + (2)(4) + (2)(-4)$$
$$= 16 + 6 + 8 - 8 = 22 \text{ gallons (net change)}$$

Apply the net change theorem:

$$Q(8) \approx Q(0) + 22 = 500 + 22 = 522 \text{ gallons}$$
Rubric note

The negative value at $t = 6$ matters. The net rate at that interval is $-4$ gallons per hour, meaning water is leaving faster than it is entering. Dropping that negative sign overestimates the tank level by 16 gallons and will cause an error on any justification question that follows. Always write out the net rate at each endpoint before summing.

Worked Example 2: Formula-Based, All 4 Parts

Cars enter a parking lot at rate $E(t) = 60 + 2t$ cars per hour and exit at rate $X(t) = 10t$ cars per hour, for $0 \leq t \leq 6$ hours after the lot opens. At $t = 0$, the lot has 50 cars.

Part (a): Find $N(t)$, the number of cars in the lot at time $t$.

Set up the net change theorem:

$$N(t) = N(0) + \int_0^t [E(u) - X(u)]\,du$$
$$= 50 + \int_0^t [(60 + 2u) - 10u]\,du = 50 + \int_0^t (60 - 8u)\,du$$
$$= 50 + \left[60u - 4u^2\right]_0^t$$
$$N(t) = 50 + 60t - 4t^2$$
Rubric language for Part (a)

"Sets up the integral of the net rate with the correct initial condition. Evaluates the antiderivative correctly." Two points: 1 for the integral setup with initial value, 1 for correct evaluation. Forgetting to add $N(0) = 50$ costs 1 point.

Part (b): When is the parking lot fullest during $0 \leq t \leq 6$?

The lot is fullest when the net rate changes from positive to negative. Set $E(t) = X(t)$:

$$60 + 2t = 10t \Rightarrow 60 = 8t \Rightarrow t = 7.5$$

The critical point $t = 7.5$ falls outside the domain $[0, 6]$. Check whether the net rate is positive or negative on the entire interval. At $t = 6$: $E(6) = 72$ and $X(6) = 60$, so $E(6) - X(6) = 12 > 0$. The net rate is positive on the entire domain. The lot is always gaining cars, so the maximum occurs at $t = 6$.

$$N(6) = 50 + 60(6) - 4(36) = 50 + 360 - 144 = 266 \text{ cars}$$
Rubric language for Part (b)

"Sets $E(t) = X(t)$, finds $t = 7.5$, notes this is outside the domain. States that $E(t) > X(t)$ for all $t$ in $[0, 6]$, so $N(t)$ is increasing on the entire interval. Concludes the maximum is at $t = 6$." The justification earns its own point separate from the numerical answer. Writing only "266 cars at $t = 6$" without the sign argument will lose a point.

Part (c): How many total cars entered the lot from $t = 0$ to $t = 6$?

This asks about total cars that entered, not the net change. Integrate $E(t)$ only:

$$\int_0^6 E(t)\,dt = \int_0^6 (60 + 2t)\,dt = \left[60t + t^2\right]_0^6 = 360 + 36 = 396 \text{ cars}$$

396 cars entered the lot from $t = 0$ to $t = 6$.

The trap students fall into: After working parts (a) and (b) with the net rate, many students automatically integrate $[E(t) - X(t)]$ here. That gives net change (how many more cars are in the lot), not total count of cars that entered. Read the question word for word. "Total cars that entered" means integrate $E(t)$ alone.

Part (d): Is the lot gaining or losing cars at $t = 3$? Justify.

Evaluate the net rate at $t = 3$:

$$E(3) - X(3) = (60 + 6) - 30 = 66 - 30 = 36$$

Since $E(3) - X(3) = 36 > 0$, the net rate of change of cars in the lot is positive at $t = 3$. Therefore the lot is gaining cars at $t = 3$.

Rubric language for Part (d)

The AP grader is looking for three things: (1) the numerical value of the net rate at the given time, (2) the comparison to zero, and (3) the interpretation in context. Write all three in one or two sentences. "$36 > 0$" alone does not earn the justification point. "The lot is gaining cars because $E(3) - X(3) = 36 > 0$" does.

The 3 Mistakes That Cost Points

Mistake 1: Integrating when the problem specifies a Riemann sum

When the problem gives you a table and says "use a left Riemann sum with 4 subintervals," that is not a suggestion. It is the method you are being scored on. The problem is specifically testing whether you can set up and evaluate a Riemann sum from tabular data. If you try to approximate a formula from the table and integrate it, you lose the setup point and the grader cannot award the consistency point. Use exactly what the problem asks.

Mistake 2: Confusing net change with total accumulated

This costs a point on nearly every accumulation FRQ. Net change answers "how much more is in the tank than at the start." Total accumulated answers "how much flowed in, regardless of what also left." When you see "total amount that entered," integrate the inflow rate only. When you see "amount in the tank at time $t$," use the net change theorem with both rates. The distinction is always in the phrasing of the question. Read slowly.

Mistake 3: Finding the critical time but skipping the justification

Setting $R_{in}(t) = R_{out}(t)$ and solving for $t$ earns one point. The justification that the net rate changes from positive to negative at that time earns another. Students find the time and stop. To earn the justification point, write: "Because $R_{in}(t) > R_{out}(t)$ for $t < t^*$ and $R_{in}(t) < R_{out}(t)$ for $t > t^*$, the net rate changes from positive to negative at $t = t^*$, so the quantity has a maximum at $t = t^*$." That sentence is worth more points than the algebra that found $t^*$.

Practice Problems

Problem 1

A bathtub holds 80 gallons at $t = 0$. Water enters at $R_{in}(t) = 4$ gallons per minute and drains at $R_{out}(t) = t$ gallons per minute for $0 \leq t \leq 8$ minutes. Find the amount of water in the tub at $t = 4$.

Problem 2

The table below shows the rate at which people enter and leave a museum (people per hour) at selected times.

$t$ (hours)Entry rateExit rate
05010
14520
24035
33040

At $t = 0$, there are 100 people in the museum. Use a left Riemann sum with 3 subintervals to estimate the number of people in the museum at $t = 3$.

Problem 3

Oil flows into a tank at rate $I(t) = 12t$ gallons per hour and out at rate $O(t) = 3t^2$ gallons per hour for $0 \leq t \leq 6$. At $t = 0$, there are 200 gallons in the tank. At what time is the tank fullest? Justify your answer.

Answers
Problem 1: $Q(4) = 80 + \int_0^4 (4 - t)\,dt = 80 + \left[4t - \frac{t^2}{2}\right]_0^4 = 80 + (16 - 8) = 88$ gallons.
Problem 2: Net rates at left endpoints: $50-10=40$, $45-20=25$, $40-35=5$. Left sum: $(1)(40)+(1)(25)+(1)(5) = 70$. Museum at $t=3 \approx 100 + 70 = 170$ people.
Problem 3: Set $I(t) = O(t)$: $12t = 3t^2 \Rightarrow t(12-3t)=0 \Rightarrow t=4$. On $(0,4)$: $I(t) > O(t)$ (gaining). On $(4,6)$: $I(t) < O(t)$ (losing). Net rate changes from positive to negative at $t=4$, so the tank is fullest at $t=4$. $N(4) = 200 + \int_0^4 (12t-3t^2)\,dt = 200 + [6t^2-t^3]_0^4 = 200 + (96-64) = 232$ gallons.

FAQ

Q What is the net change theorem in AP Calculus? +

The net change theorem states that the integral of a rate of change over an interval gives the total change in the quantity. In accumulation problems: starting amount plus the integral of (rate in minus rate out) equals the amount at time $b$. Written as $Q(b) = Q(a) + \int_a^b [R_{in}(t) - R_{out}(t)]\,dt$. Every rate in/rate out FRQ on the AP exam is a direct application of this one equation.

Q How do I know when to use a Riemann sum instead of integrating? +

Use a Riemann sum when the problem gives you a table of values and specifies which sum to use (left, right, or midpoint). The problem will state this explicitly, usually as "use a left Riemann sum with [n] subintervals." When the problem gives you formulas for the rate functions, evaluate the integral exactly. Never mix the two approaches on the same problem part.

Q What is the difference between net change and total accumulated? +

Net change is the integral of (rate in minus rate out). It tells you how much more or less of the quantity exists compared to the starting value. Total accumulated is the integral of the inflow rate alone. It tells you how much of the quantity entered the system, regardless of what also left. The AP exam frequently asks both in the same problem with different phrasing, specifically to test whether you know which integral to set up.

Q How do I find when a quantity is at its maximum in a rate in/rate out problem? +

Set the inflow rate equal to the outflow rate and solve for $t$. Verify that the net rate changes from positive to negative at that point (positive to negative is a maximum; negative to positive is a minimum). If the critical point is outside the domain, compare endpoint values instead. Always include a written justification citing the sign change of the net rate. That justification earns its own rubric point separate from the calculation.
